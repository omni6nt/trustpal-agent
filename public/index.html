Four parts. Each one has a limit.

Owner

  • Approves payments
  • Sets the limits and the split
  • Pauses everything
  • Must keep the owner key safe: it is the master key

AI agent

  • Reads invoices
  • Proposes payments
  • Sends payments the owner approved
  • Cannot approve
  • Cannot change limits or the split
  • Cannot add keys or send money freely

Guard

  • Flags look-alike and blocked addresses
  • Explains why in plain words
  • Cannot move money
  • Is advisory: the contract does not depend on it

NEAR contract

  • Holds the money
  • Checks every rule, every time
  • Cannot be talked into skipping a rule

Where it stands

Trustpal is a testnet prototype with open, tested code: 31 contract tests, 55 application tests and a published red-team run. It pays in native NEAR today, with stablecoins next, and an outside audit comes before any real funds. It has been through several independent AI security reviews, with the findings fixed. The full list is in the defence note.

Questions, answered.

Can the AI take my money?

It holds one key that can only call pay. It cannot change your rules or approve its own exceptions. The contract refuses anyone who is not on your list unless you approved that exact payment.

What if the AI is fooled?

It can still be fooled into proposing a wrong payment. The contract's limits cap the damage, and a payment that does not match the invoice total waits for you. In our red-team run, money moved wrongly 0 times.

Do I need a wallet to try it?

No. Practice mode runs on your computer with no wallet and no money. Live mode is one configured demo business on NEAR testnet.

Can anyone sign up with their own rules?

Not yet. That needs a separate treasury for every wallet, which we have left for after the hackathon.
